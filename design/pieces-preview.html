Chess piece rendering — before vs. after

Two boards on top show the current Unicode-glyph approach on a desktop-sized board (480 px) and on a mobile-sized board (320 px). The same two boards below use the proposed inline SVG (Cburnett) approach. Same starting position, same palette.

On desktop the difference is modest — the Unicode outline trick more-or-less holds up. On mobile it collapses: Android replaces the glyphs with color emoji entirely, and iOS's monochrome glyphs get blurred by the fake text-shadow outline. The SVG version renders identically everywhere.

What this preview does not show. The current Unicode rendering above uses a desktop text-glyph fallback that's the best case for the current approach — on your actual mobile browsers it looks worse (color-emoji cartoons on Android, muddy shadow on iOS).
