Five candidates. Read the top strips left-to-right — the same five favicons at the sizes your browser and OS will actually use (16 px in a tab, 32 px in a bookmark bar, 64 px on a home screen shortcut, 180 px as an Apple touch icon). Below, each design gets its own card with a bigger preview and a "modernization level" bar so you can see the spectrum.

Level

1 · Vintage line-art knight

The Cburnett knight rendered as an old-book illustration — cream tile, thin forest border, black line-art. Familiar to anyone who's used Lichess or opened a chess book. Rewards larger sizes; at 16 px the detail compresses to a small silhouette but still reads as a knight.

Best if you want the favicon to feel like a chess reference book on the shelf.

Level
2/5

2 · Editorial serif H

A bold Georgia-style serif H on cream with a gold underscore and two tiny chess-square dots on either side of the crossbar. Reads as a magazine/masthead mark; the chess link is subtle but present.

Best if you want it to feel more like a private study/journal than a game.

Level
3/5

3 · Contemporary — piece on chessboard

A simplified knight silhouette on a 2×2 chessboard tile — cream and forest squares, gold shadow behind the piece. Bridges the two halves of the spectrum: instantly readable as chess, but with a cleaner geometric base than option 1.

Best if you want "chess" to be obvious without leaning vintage.

Level
4/5

4 · Modern flat silhouette

A single bold knight silhouette in gold on a forest-green rounded square. Two colours, no outline, no detail. Reads perfectly at 16 px because there's nothing to lose in translation. Feels like a product-app icon on a phone home screen.

Best if you want it to look like a proper 2020s app icon.

Level
5/5

5 · Abstract — the "hanging" mark

A rounded forest tile with a small cream square (a "chess square") and a bold gold dot floating just off it — the piece is hanging. No literal chess piece anywhere. Reads as a modern brand mark; the meaning unfolds after you know what the app is.

Best if you want it to feel like a startup / product identity that doesn't shout "chess".

Rendering. All five are inline SVG in this page. Whichever you pick, we'd save it as src/hanging_piece_trainer/static/favicon.svg and link it from every template (<link rel="icon" type="image/svg+xml" href="…">). Modern browsers use the SVG directly at every size; for older Safari we'd add a small favicon.ico derived from the 32 px version.

Palette. All designs use the site's existing tokens: --paper #f4f1e8, --forest #234b3a, --gold #d7a928, --light-square #e9dfca, --dark-square #66806b. No new colours are introduced.
